# The state layer for Section 138 (cheque bounce) — **Kerala**

*A practitioner-oriented map of the Kerala-specific rules, court practice and
execution machinery that sit on top of the uniform s.138 core. Not legal advice;
verify every instrument against the current notification before relying on it.*

---

## 0. Read this first — what "state layer" means here

**Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881 is *central* law and is
identical in Kerala and everywhere else in India.** There is **no Kerala
amendment to s.138** (or to ss.139–147). So "state amendments, rules and laws"
for a cheque-bounce case in Kerala does **not** mean a different offence — it
means the *procedural and administrative layer* that decides **how** the case is
filed, tried, and — the part you asked about — **how summons and warrants are
issued and executed, and where the police come in.**

That layer, for Kerala, is made of five things:

1. the **court-procedure code** (since 1 July 2024, the **BNSS**, which replaced the CrPC) — central, but applied through Kerala courts;
2. Kerala's **Criminal Rules of Practice, 1982** and the **Rules of the High Court of Kerala**;
3. Kerala's **e-courts / e-filing rules** and the High Court's **circulars and case-management practice**;
4. the **Kerala Police Act, 2011** (who executes warrants and how); and
5. the **court language** (Malayalam) and its knock-on rules for summons and notices.

Binding **Supreme Court directions** on s.138 procedure apply in Kerala the same
as everywhere (they are the ceiling the Kerala instruments operate under).

---

## 1. The procedure code and the 2024 switch (applied in Kerala)

A s.138 case is a **complaint case tried as a summons case** (with a summary-trial
overlay under **s.143 NI Act**). The procedural spine used to be the CrPC; for any
cause of action / step on or after **1 July 2024** it is the **BNSS**. The map
that matters for summons/warrants/police:

| Step in a s.138 case | Old CrPC | Now (BNSS, 2023) |
|---|---|---|
| Magistrate takes cognizance on the complaint | s.190 | **s.210** |
| Examination of complainant on oath | s.200 | **s.223** — *new first proviso: accused to be given an opportunity of being heard before cognizance* |
| Postponement of process / **mandatory inquiry where the accused lives outside the court's jurisdiction** | s.202 | **s.225** |
| Issue of **summons** to the accused | s.204 | **s.227** |
| Dispensing with personal attendance of the accused | s.205 | **s.228** |
| Form & **service of summons** | ss.61–69 | **ss.63–71** (incl. electronic-form summons) |
| **Warrants** of arrest (incl. bailable / non-bailable) | ss.70–81 | **ss.72–83** |
| **Proclamation & attachment** for an absconding accused | ss.82–83 | **ss.84–85** |
| Electronic conduct of proceedings / service | — | **s.530 / s.532** (trials, inquiries and *service* may be in electronic mode) |
| NI-Act-specific: **service of summons** | *NI Act s.144* (speed post / approved courier; deemed service on refusal) | unchanged |
| NI-Act-specific: **evidence of complainant on affidavit** | *NI Act s.145* | unchanged |
| NI-Act-specific: **summary trial** | *NI Act s.143* | unchanged |

> **Kerala practice point.** Kerala courts apply the BNSS mapping above, but the
> *form* of the process, the registers, and the mechanics of service are governed
> by Kerala's own Criminal Rules of Practice (§2) and High Court circulars (§3).

## 2. Kerala's rules of practice

### 2.1 Criminal Rules of Practice, Kerala, 1982
The core state instrument for how a criminal court in Kerala issues and serves
process. The rules directly relevant to a s.138 summons/warrant:

- **Rule 7 – Signing of summons.** Summonses to accused and witnesses are ordinarily signed by the Chief Ministerial Officer, "By order of the Court."
- **Rules 8–12 – Service on special classes of witness** — medical witnesses (8, 12), official witnesses whose address is unknown, served through the head of department (9), **police officers, summoned through their superior officer** (10), and the Director, Forensic Science Laboratory (11). (Relevant when the bank official / handwriting expert is a witness.)
- **Rule 13 – Form of summons.** Distinguished persons are summoned in **Judicial Form No. 2**.
- **Rule 14 – Translation of process.** Process in a language other than the official language requires an authorised **English translation** — the hook for the Malayalam / language issues in §5.
- **Rules 72–73 – Registers.** The court **diary** (Administrative Form No. 10) and **proceedings paper** (Judicial Form No. 61) — the case-record spine.
- The Rules also carry the **forms and execution mechanics for warrants and for proclamation/attachment** used when an accused does not answer summons (§3.3).

### 2.2 Rules of the High Court of Kerala
Govern the High Court's own side (appeals, revisions, and criminal miscellaneous
petitions — e.g. quashing a s.138 complaint under s.482 CrPC / **s.528 BNSS**, or a
revision against conviction). Relevant because a large share of s.138 litigation
in Kerala is the **quashing / revision** layer at the High Court.

### 2.3 Civil Rules of Practice, Kerala
Not the trial track for s.138 (which is criminal), but relevant where a parallel
**summary suit** or execution is run for the same debt.

## 3. E-courts, service of summons, and the warrant ladder — *the summons/warrant/police core*

### 3.1 E-filing and case management
- **Electronic Filing Rules for Courts (Kerala), 2021** — the enabling rules for e-filing in Kerala courts.
- **SOP for e-Filing in the District Judiciary through the DCMS portal** (High Court of Kerala, **9 July 2025**) — the current operating procedure; filing at `filing.keralacourts.in`. *Note: this governs how a s.138 complaint is filed, not how summons/warrants are served electronically.*

### 3.2 Service of summons on the accused
- **Primary route:** **NI Act s.144** — the summons may be served by **speed post or an approved courier**, and is **deemed served even if the accused refuses** to accept it. This is the fast track the SC has repeatedly told magistrates to use for s.138.
- **General route:** **BNSS ss.63–71** (was CrPC 61–69), including summons in **electronic form**, plus **BNSS s.532** allowing service in electronic mode.
- **Kerala's conservative line on *informal* e-service.** In *Anoop Jacob* (**Justice V.G. Arun**), the Kerala High Court held that **"law does not provide for service of summons via WhatsApp"** and **kept a non-bailable warrant in abeyance** because service had been attempted over WhatsApp rather than by a legally recognised mode. Contrast this with High Courts that have allowed email/WhatsApp service (e.g. Uttarakhand) — **so in Kerala, plan for statutory modes (s.144 NI Act / BNSS) and treat WhatsApp/email as insufficient on their own** until Kerala notifies dedicated electronic-service rules (it has notified **e-filing** rules but, as of this writing, **not** a general electronic-service-of-process rule).

### 3.3 The warrant ladder (non-appearance)
When the accused does not appear after summons, the escalation Kerala magistrates
run is:

1. **Summons** (BNSS 227 / NI Act 144) →
2. **Bailable warrant** →
3. **Non-bailable warrant (NBW)** (BNSS ss.72–83) →
4. **Proclamation and attachment** of property for an absconder (BNSS ss.84–85).

Kerala courts follow the Supreme Court's caution against **mechanical issuance of
NBWs**; the *Anoop Jacob* order above is an example of a Kerala NBW being stayed
for defective service.

### 3.4 Where the police come in — Kerala Police Act, 2011
- A s.138 case is a **complaint case**, **not** an FIR/investigation case: there is **no police investigation, no arrest at the outset**, and the payee (not the State) prosecutes. The police enter only at the **execution** stage.
- **Execution of warrants and service of court process** is a duty of the police under the **Kerala Police Act, 2011** (the Station House Officer's duties), read with BNSS warrant provisions. When a magistrate issues a **bailable/non-bailable warrant** in a s.138 case, it is the **local SHO** who executes it.
- Practically: complainant's counsel obtains the warrant → it is routed to the **jurisdictional police station** → the SHO effects arrest/production or reports non-execution → escalates to proclamation/attachment.

## 4. Special courts and disposal mechanisms in Kerala

- **Designated magistrate courts.** s.138 complaints are tried by **Judicial First Class Magistrates**; in the metros/high-volume stations the High Court has concentrated NI Act work before specified magistrates for faster disposal (per the Supreme Court's *In re Expeditious Trial* direction to set up **pilot special courts**).
- **Lok Adalats / Adalats.** Kerala makes heavy use of **Lok Adalats and mega Lok Adalats** (Kerala State Legal Services Authority) to settle/compound s.138 cases — compounding is expressly permitted by **s.147 NI Act**, and the SC (*Damodar S. Prabhu*) encourages early settlement.
- **e-Courts.** Case status and cause-lists via the Kerala district judiciary e-courts portal; hybrid/virtual hearings under BNSS s.530/532.

---

## 5. Court language

- The working language of Kerala's subordinate courts is **Malayalam** (with English at the High Court). This drives **Rule 14, CRP 1982** (translation of process) and affects the **language of the summons, the demand-notice evidence, and any e-filing UI**. For a platform serving Kerala, summons/notice generation and the litigant-facing interface need Malayalam.

## 6. How the Kerala High Court reads s.138 (procedural posture)

Not "amendments," but the **Kerala-specific case law** a s.138 practitioner in
Kerala works under (subject to Supreme Court authority above it):

- **Jurisdiction** — the court where the **cheque was presented for collection** (payee's bank) has jurisdiction (Kerala HC, applying s.142(2) NI Act as amended in 2015).
- **Demand notice must specify the dishonoured amount** — a notice that omits the specific cheque amount is defective (Kerala HC).
- **Cash loans above ₹20,000** — the Kerala HC had held a s.138 case not maintainable for an unexplained cash debt over ₹20,000 (s.269SS Income-tax Act line); **this was *set aside* by the Supreme Court**, which held the case *is* maintainable. *(Good example of a Kerala HC position that did not survive — flag it as overruled.)*
- **Closing the case on payment** — a s.138 case can be closed where the convict pays the fine/compensation directly to the complainant (Kerala HC).
- **K. Bhaskaran v. Sankaran Vaidhyan Balan** — the foundational s.138 jurisdiction/notice authority, itself a **Kerala-origin** appeal to the Supreme Court.

---

## 7. What is *not* Kerala-specific (so you don't over-model it)

- **The offence and its ingredients** (s.138), the **presumptions** (ss.118/139), **notice period and limitation**, **compounding** (s.147), **interim compensation** (s.143A) and **appeal deposit** (s.148) — all **central and uniform**; Kerala adds nothing.
- **Binding Supreme Court directions** that Kerala courts must follow: *In re Expeditious Trial of Cases u/s 138* (2021), *Indian Bank Assn v. Union of India* (2014), and the 2025 backlog-disposal guidelines (*Sanjabij Tari v. Kishore S. Borcar* line) — national, not Kerala instruments.
